Why Encoder-Decoders? ­ The Seq2Seq Imperative

· The Core Problem: Many NLP tasks are inherently sequence-to-sequence:


  · - Translation: " "  "Hello"


  · - Summarization: [Long Document]  [Short Summary]


  · - Generative QA: "Context: ... Question: ..."  "Answer string"

· Encoder Limitation: Reads bidirectionally but cannot generate output

   autoregressively.


· Decoder Limitation: Generates autoregressively but processes input causally

   (cannot deeply encode full input rst).


· Encoder-Decoder Solution: Encode the full input bidirectionally rst, then

   generate output autoregressively via Cross-Attention.

BART Corruption Strategy 1 ­ Token Masking & Deletion

· Token Masking (like BERT):

  · Random tokens replaced with `[MASK]`.

  · Input: "The cat <mask> on the mat"  Target: "The cat sat on the mat"


· Token Deletion (unique to BART):

  · Random tokens are deleted entirely (no placeholder left behind).

  · Input: "The cat on the mat"  Target: "The cat sat on the mat"

  · Challenge: Model must detect what is missing AND where it was deleted.


· Tensor Impact:

  · Masking: `src_len = tgt_len`

  · Deletion: `src_len < tgt_len`
BART Corruption Strategy 2 ­ Text In lling

· Text In lling (BART's signature corruption):

  · Contiguous spans of tokens replaced with a single [MASK] token.

  · Span lengths drawn from Poisson distribution ( = 3).

  · Input: "The <mask> mat <mask> happily" (replaces "cat sat on the")

  · Target: "The cat sat on the mat happily"


· Critical Challenge:

  · Each [MASK] represents 0 or more tokens.

  · Model must learn to predict how many tokens each mask replaces.


· Math Note: Span length  Poisson( = 3). 0-length spans create token insertions.

BART Corruption Strategy 3 ­ Sentence Permutation & Rotation

· Sentence Permutation:

  · Document split into sentences. Sentences shu ed randomly.

  · Model learns document-level coherence and discourse structure.

  · Input: "[S3] [S1] [S4] [S2]"  Target: "[S1] [S2] [S3] [S4]"


· Document Rotation:

  · A random token is chosen as the pivot. Document is rotated to begin there.

  · Model must identify the true start of the document.

  · Input: "sat on the mat. The cat"  Target: "The cat sat on the mat."


· Combined E ect: Teaches global document structure, not just local dependencies.

T5 ­ Span Corruption Mechanics

· Span Corruption Step-by-Step:

   · 1. Randomly select non-overlapping spans (1 or more tokens).

   · 2. Replace each span with a unique sentinel: <extra_id_0>, <extra_id_1>, ...

   · 3. Encoder Input: Corrupted text with sentinels.

   · 4. Decoder Target: ONLY the dropped spans, preceded by corresponding sentinels.


· Example:

   · Original: "The cute cat sat on the fuzzy mat"

   · Input: "The <extra_id_0> cat sat on the <extra_id_1>" # src_len = 7

   · Target: "<extra_id_0> cute <extra_id_1> fuzzy mat <extra_id_2> </s>"

      # tgt_len = 7 vs. original 8 vs. BART 10


· Insight: Decoder target is MUCH shorter than original text!

T5 vs. BART ­ Compute E ciency Argument

· The Mathematical Argument: Target Length E ciency

· BART (Full Reconstruction):


   · Target: N tokens.

   · Decoder Self-Attention FLOPs  O(N2).

· T5 (Span Corruption):

   · Target:  0.20N tokens (15% corrupted + 5% sentinels).

   · Decoder Self-Attention FLOPs  O((0.2N)2) = O(0.04N2).

· Speedup Factor:

   · Decoder self-attention is  25 × cheaper (since (1/0.2)2 = 25).

   · Overall training speedup  2 - 3 × (Encoder cost remains similar).

· Takeaway: T5 enables training on vastly more data within the same compute budget.

Why Frontier Models Converged on Decoder-Only

· Three Structural Reasons:

· 1. Serving Simplicity & Parameter E ciency.

· 2. Natural Fit for Multi-turn Alignment (RLHF).

· 3. Predictable & Linear KV Cache.

· The Scaling Driver:


  · At massive scale (>100B params), single-stream In-Context Learning and

      Chain-of-Thought unlock superior multi-step reasoning.

Reason 1 ­ Serving Simplicity & Parameter E ciency

· Parameter Duplication Problem:

  · Enc-Dec with P params:  P/2 in Encoder,  P/2 in Decoder.

  · Dec-Only with P params: All P dedicated to generation.


· Serving Complexity:

  · Enc-Dec requires TWO distinct routing passes. This causes an Enc-Dec

      Penalty: it breaks continuous batching and destroys kernel fusion
      opportunities (due to CPU-GPU synchronization overhead).


  · Dec-Only is ONE uni ed loop. Easier to batch and optimize.

Reason 2 ­ Natural Fit for Multi-turn Alignment & RLHF

· RLHF/DPO Pipeline (Week 11):

  · AI alignment relies on 

      rewarding generated tokens 
      in conversation formats.


· Decoder-Only Advantage:

  · Single sequence: `<system>...<user>...<assistant>...`

  · Easy to calculate reward gradients over the whole continuous context.


· Enc-Dec Challenge:

  · Where does the reward gradient go? History needs to be re-encoded at

      every conversational turn, confusing the signal.
Reason 3 ­ Predictable & Linear KV Cache Scalability

· Decoder-Only KV Cache:

  · Linear growth: `[batch, seq_len, d_head]`. Predictable memory

      footprint.


  · Enables PagedAttention (vLLM) and Chunked Pre ll & Pre x Caching

      (e.g., RadixAttention), crucial for RAG.


· Encoder-Decoder KV Cache:

  · Two Caches: Static Encoder Cache (`src_len`) + Dynamic Decoder

      Cache (`tgt_len` growing).


  · Complicates continuous batching and memory paging.

   understanding.

   (drastic compute savings).

   inputoutput string format.

   abstractive summarization.
